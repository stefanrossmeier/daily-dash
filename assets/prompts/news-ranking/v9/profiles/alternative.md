# Profile: Alternative News

Find financially relevant developments and analysis that can add information,
evidence or perspectives likely to be missed or underweighted by mainstream
financial-news coverage.

The normal market-impact hierarchy still applies.

Give additional value to:

- original reporting;
- independent analysis based on evidence;
- developments receiving limited mainstream attention despite plausible
  financial significance;
- credible contrarian interpretations;
- emerging risks;
- second-order effects;
- structural economic or geopolitical developments.

Do not reward a story merely because it is contrarian, anti-consensus or from
an alternative source.

Penalize:

- unsupported speculation;
- ideological commentary without new information;
- sensationalism;
- conspiracy claims without credible evidence;
- recycled mainstream stories presented as alternative insight.

A well-supported overlooked Tier 4 story should rank highly.

An unsupported dramatic claim should rank low regardless of how unusual it is.

## Selection discipline

Alternative value is a bonus only after financial materiality is established.
A story must not be selected merely because it is unusual, contrarian, local,
politically charged or absent from mainstream headlines.

For `selected: true`, require both:

1. a plausible, headline-supported path to material investment, macroeconomic,
   sector, policy, funding, commodity, rates, FX or risk implications; and
2. meaningful alternative value, such as overlooked evidence, a credible
   second-order effect, an emerging risk or a differentiated interpretation
   that adds something beyond routine market coverage.

Prefer `selected: false` when the financial transmission is mainly speculative,
confined to one municipality or narrow entity, or depends on imagining facts
that are not present in the headline. Novelty alone is not enough.

Likewise, ordinary mainstream macro or market updates do not become Alternative
News merely because they are financially relevant. They should be selected here
only when the headline itself supplies a differentiated implication or
underweighted angle.
