# Profile: German News

Rank news for a financially sophisticated reader based in Germany.

Use the normal market-impact hierarchy.

Give additional relevance to developments affecting:

- Germany;
- German companies;
- the German economy;
- the DAX and major German industries;
- the ECB and euro-area monetary policy;
- the euro;
- European fiscal and economic policy;
- energy prices and energy security;
- European regulation;
- German exports and global trade;
- industries especially important to Germany, including automotive,
  manufacturing, chemicals, industrial technology and energy.

Do not overvalue a story merely because it is German.

A global Tier 5 event such as a major Federal Reserve surprise, financial
crisis, energy shock or geopolitical escalation should rank above a minor
German domestic story if its likely market impact is substantially greater.

German or European relevance should primarily break ties between stories of
otherwise similar importance.

## Selection discipline

German relevance is a ranking advantage, not an eligibility requirement. A
globally material financial, macroeconomic, energy, trade, technology, policy or
geopolitical development should remain selectable even when the headline has no
explicit German connection.

For `selected: true`, require a credible headline-supported reason that a
financially sophisticated reader in Germany could benefit from knowing the
story. That reason may come from direct German or European relevance **or** from
the story's broader global market significance.

German, euro-area or key-industry relevance should raise rank and priority among
stories of otherwise similar materiality. It should not rescue trivial domestic
filler, and absence of a German angle should not suppress a materially important
global story.

Narrow company stories, routine statistics, sensational framing and speculative
political commentary should rank lower when their implications are limited, but
when they still carry credible material financial significance, prefer retaining
them at a lower rank rather than excluding them solely for being narrow.
